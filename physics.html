ARTICLE 01 • THE BASICS

What Exactly Is Physics?

Have you ever wondered why the sky is blue, how a rainbow forms, or why objects fall towards the ground? The answers to these questions can be found in physics!

Physics is a branch of science that studies matter, energy, motion and the forces that act on objects. It helps us understand how the universe works, from tiny atoms to enormous galaxies.

Physics is also a part of our everyday lives. Electricity powers our homes, sound allows us to communicate, and the laws of motion help us understand how vehicles travel. By studying physics, we learn to question, experiment and discover the science behind ordinary things.

ARTICLE 02 • NEWTON'S LAWS

Newton's First Law of Motion

Imagine sitting in a moving bus. When the driver suddenly applies the brakes, your body tends to move forward. But why does that happen? This is where Newton's First Law comes in!

Newton's First Law states that an object remains at rest or continues moving in a straight line at a constant speed unless acted upon by an unbalanced external force.

This tendency of objects to resist changes in their state of motion is called inertia. For example, a football lying on the ground will remain still until someone kicks it. Similarly, a moving bicycle slows down because of forces such as friction and air resistance.

In simple words, objects do not change their motion on their own. A force is needed to change it.

ARTICLE 03 • NEWTON'S LAWS

Newton's Second Law of Motion

Why is it easier to push an empty shopping trolley than a fully loaded one? And why does a harder push make an object speed up more? Newton's Second Law explains this relationship!

Newton's Second Law explains how the acceleration of an object depends on the net force acting on it and its mass. A greater net force produces greater acceleration when the mass stays the same.

However, if the same force is applied to two objects, the object with less mass will have greater acceleration. This is why an empty trolley is easier to accelerate than a loaded one.

This law is commonly written as F = ma, where F represents net force, m represents mass, and a represents acceleration.

ARTICLE 04 • NEWTON'S LAWS

Newton's Third Law of Motion

Have you ever wondered how a rocket moves upwards into space? It may seem like magic, but it is actually an amazing example of Newton's Third Law!

Newton's Third Law states that for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. This means that forces always occur in pairs.

When a rocket releases hot gases downwards, the gases exert an equal and opposite force on the rocket, pushing it upwards. The two forces act on different objects, which is why they do not cancel each other out.

We can also see this law when we walk. Our feet push the ground backwards, and the ground pushes us forwards. This interaction helps us move!
