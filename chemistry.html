01 / THE BASICS

What Exactly Is Chemistry?

Chemistry is the branch of science that studies matter, what it is made of, its properties, and how it changes. It helps us understand the materials around us and the reactions that happen every day.

Matter is anything that has mass and occupies space. Water, air, soil, plants and even the devices we use are made of matter.

Chemistry also explains how different substances interact. When two or more substances react, they may form completely new substances with different properties.

02 / ATOMIC WORLD

Atoms – The Tiny Building Blocks

Everything around us is made up of tiny particles called atoms. Atoms are the basic building blocks of ordinary matter. They are so small that we cannot see them with our naked eyes.

Every atom has a central nucleus containing protons and neutrons. Electrons occupy the space around the nucleus.

Different types of atoms make up different elements. For example, hydrogen, oxygen and carbon are all made of their own kinds of atoms.

03 / UNDERSTANDING MATTER

Elements, Compounds and Mixtures

Matter can be classified into different types. Elements, compounds and mixtures are three important ways of understanding the substances around us.

An element contains only one type of atom. Oxygen and iron are examples of elements.

A compound forms when two or more elements chemically combine in a fixed proportion. Water is a compound made from hydrogen and oxygen.

A mixture contains two or more substances physically combined. Air and salt water are examples of mixtures.

04 / EVERYDAY CHEMISTRY

Acids, Bases and Indicators

Acids and bases are two important groups of substances. Lemons contain citric acid, while substances like soap are usually basic.

Acids generally have a pH below 7, while bases have a pH above 7. A neutral substance, such as pure water at room temperature, has a pH close to 7.

Indicators help us identify whether a substance is acidic or basic. Litmus paper is a common indicator: acids turn blue litmus red, while bases turn red litmus blue.
